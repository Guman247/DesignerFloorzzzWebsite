How We Do It

Two days, no shortcuts

There are a lot of garage floor companies in the Tampa Bay area, and most of them will tell you the same things: durable, beautiful, done in a day. We do things a little differently, and we would rather explain why than just ask you to trust us.

This page walks through exactly how we install a flake garage floor, what each layer is for, why we use an epoxy base coat instead of a fast-cure polyurea, and why we broadcast flake until the floor cannot hold any more. If you are comparing quotes, it should help you ask better questions of everyone, including us.

Our Process

The Two-Day Install

Day 1 · Morning

Grind and test

We diamond grind the entire slab with 30-grit tooling. This strips old paint, sealers and the weak top layer of the concrete and opens up a rough profile the coating can lock into. Then we moisture test the slab before anything goes down.

Day 1 · Midday

Repair and seal

Cracks and control joints get filled with XPS crack filler and ground flush. If the moisture test calls for it, we apply a moisture vapor barrier (MVB) and adjust the schedule so it cures fully before the base coat.

Day 1 · Afternoon

Base coat and full broadcast

We roll out the XPS epoxy base coat and broadcast flake into it until the surface will not accept any more. Then it cures overnight. This cure is the step one-day installs skip.

Day 2

Scrape, vacuum, topcoat

We scrape the loose flake, vacuum the floor clean and apply the XPS polyaspartic topcoat. You can walk on it about 18 hours later and park on it in 48 to 72 hours.

What You Are Paying For

The System, Layer by Layer

  1. Ground concrete. A 30-grit diamond grind removes the weak surface layer and leaves a rough profile. Coatings do not bond to smooth concrete; they bond to that profile. Acid etching, which some crews use because it is faster, does not open the concrete the same way.
  2. Crack and joint repair. Cracks and control joints are filled with XPS crack filler and ground flush. A crack that is simply coated over will telegraph straight through the new floor.
  3. Moisture vapor barrier, when needed. Florida slabs sit on wet ground. Water vapor pushes up through concrete constantly, and when it hits the underside of a coating it builds pressure until the coating lifts. If the moisture test shows the slab is pushing vapor, the MVB goes down first and blocks it.
  4. XPS epoxy base coat. The anchor layer. It soaks into the ground profile and hardens there, which is what holds the whole system to the slab.
  5. Full flake broadcast. Vinyl flake thrown into the wet epoxy until the surface will not take any more. This gives the floor its color and texture, hides the concrete completely and adds thickness.
  6. XPS polyaspartic topcoat. The wear layer. Polyaspartic is UV stable, so it will not yellow in a sun-facing garage the way bare epoxy does, and it stands up to hot tires, oil and cleaners.

The Base Coat Question

Epoxy Base vs. Polyurea Base

Polyurea is not a bad product. We use a polyaspartic, which is a type of polyurea, as our topcoat because that is where its strengths belong. The problem is using a fast-cure polyurea as the base coat, which is how most one-day systems are built.

Epoxy base coat

  • ✓ Long working time, so it can be rolled and back-rolled into the profile
  • ✓ Slow cure lets it wet into the concrete before it hardens
  • ✓ Goes down thicker, filling the low spots of the grind
  • ✓ Cures overnight into a rigid, locked-in anchor layer

Fast-cure polyurea base

  • ✗ Can begin setting within minutes, so the crew is racing it
  • ✗ Hardens before it fully penetrates the profile
  • ✗ Thinner film with less material in the low spots
  • ✗ Flexibility is its selling point, but a base coat on a slab needs bond, not flex

The short version: a base coat has one job, which is to grab the concrete and not let go. Epoxy's slower cure is not a drawback for that job. It is the reason it works.

The Honest Part

Why many one-day floors don't last

One-day garage floors are everywhere in Tampa Bay, and plenty of them look great the day the crew leaves. The problems tend to show up in the first year or two, and they almost always trace back to the same thing: a schedule that has no room for the steps that make a coating stay down.

1. Prep gets compressed

A proper grind on a two-car garage, plus crack repair, takes hours. To fit the whole job into one day, something has to give, and it is usually the grind. A coating bonds to whatever is on top of the slab. If that is a weak, lightly etched surface, the coating comes up with it.

2. Moisture never gets tested

A moisture vapor barrier needs its own cure time. There is no room for that in a one-day schedule, so the test gets skipped and the barrier never gets offered. On a Florida slab with a high water table, that is a gamble made with your money.

3. The base coat can't soak in

A fast-cure base is chosen precisely because it hardens in minutes. That is also why it sits on top of the profile instead of in it. The bond is weaker from the first day, and it only has to fail in one spot for the peeling to start.

4. Concrete outgasses into the coating

As a slab warms up during the day, air in the concrete expands and pushes out through the surface. A coating applied at the wrong moment, with no time to let that settle, traps those bubbles as pinholes and blisters.

5. The topcoat goes over a base that isn't done curing

Layers bond best when the one underneath has finished reacting. Stacking a topcoat onto a base coat that is hours old, not a day old, is one more place for the system to let go.

Our two days are not slower work. They are the same work with an overnight cure between the base coat and the topcoat. That cure is the thing the one-day schedule removes, and it is the thing you are paying for.

The Flake Itself

Full Broadcast vs. Partial Broadcast

"Full broadcast" means we throw flake into the wet base coat until the floor physically cannot hold any more. Installers call it broadcasting to refusal. A partial or light broadcast stops well before that point, and the difference is easy to see once you know what to look for.

Full broadcast

  • ✓ Base coat is completely hidden
  • ✓ Even color and texture across the whole floor
  • ✓ Consistent slip resistance
  • ✓ Thicker wear layer, so the topcoat wears evenly
  • ✓ Hides minor concrete imperfections

Partial broadcast

  • ✗ Base coat shows through between flakes
  • ✗ Scuffs and tire marks show up in the exposed base
  • ✗ Texture is inconsistent, so grip is too
  • ✗ High spots wear first and the pattern thins over time
  • ✗ Uses less flake and less time, which is why it is cheaper

A light broadcast is a legitimate look if that is what you want. It should be a choice you make with your installer, not a shortcut you discover after the fact. Every garage floor we quote is priced as a full broadcast unless you ask for something else.

The Number One Complaint

Hot tire pickup

If you have ever seen a garage floor with two bald patches exactly where the car parks, that is hot tire pickup. In Tampa Bay the asphalt gets hot enough that tires come into the garage well above the softening point of a cheap coating. The hot tire softens the coating, the tire cools and contracts overnight, and it pulls the coating up with it.

It happens for two reasons: the coating was never bonded well to begin with, or the topcoat is too soft or too thin to take the heat. Our system addresses both. The 30-grit grind and epoxy base give the floor a mechanical bond that a tire cannot peel, and the polyaspartic topcoat is formulated to handle heat and chemicals that epoxy alone would not.

No floor coating is immune to abuse, and we will not tell you otherwise. But hot tire pickup is a symptom of a shortcut somewhere in the system, and we do not take those shortcuts.

Questions We Get

Garage Floor FAQ

When can I walk on it, and when can I park on it?

Light foot traffic after about 18 hours. Vehicles after 48 to 72 hours, depending on temperature and humidity. We will give you a specific time before we leave.

How long will the floor last?

The honest answer is that it depends on prep and use. Floors that fail early almost always fail at the bond, which is exactly what the grind, the moisture test and the epoxy base protect. Ask us about the warranty on this system when we quote your floor.

Do you do one-day installs?

No. The overnight cure between the base coat and the topcoat is the point of the two-day schedule. We would rather lose a job to a one-day company than install a floor we expect to come back and fix.

What is a moisture test, and will I need a vapor barrier?

We test the slab to see how much water vapor is pushing up through the concrete. Not every garage needs a moisture vapor barrier, and we test rather than guess. If yours does, we will show you the reading and price the MVB before any coating goes down.

My floor already has paint or an old coating on it. Is that a problem?

No. The 30-grit grind removes paint, sealers and failed coatings down to clean concrete. We do not coat over an existing floor finish.

What about hot tires?

See the section above. In short, hot tire pickup is a bond problem or a soft topcoat problem, and the epoxy base plus polyaspartic topcoat is built to handle both.

What colors and flake blends do you offer?

Dozens of blends, from subtle grays to bold mixes. We bring samples to the estimate so you can see them against your walls and in your light.
